## Theoretical Framework

A structure that guides research by relying on formal theory.
It links the concepts and provides a foundation for hypothesis.

Theoretical Framework answers two basic questions:

1. What is the problem/question?
2. Why is your approach feasible for solving the problem/answering the question?

### Importance of Theoretical Framework

- Clarifies concepts and variables
- Guides data collection and analysis
- Helps interpret findings
- Establishes relationships between variables

### Types of variables

- ##### Independent Variable (IV)

  Variable that is manipulated or categorized to observe it's effect on the dependent variable. (teaching method - online vs offline)

- ##### Dependent Variable (DV)

  The outcome or response that is measured to see the effect of the independent variable. (student performance)

- ##### Intervening Variable

also called **mediating variable** explains how or why an independent variable affects a dependent variable.

Example:
IV: Education Level
Intervening: Job Skills
DV: Income

- ##### Moderating Variable

A variable that alters the strength or direction of the relationship between the independent and dependent variables.

Example:
Age moderates the effect of social media usage on mental health.

- ##### Control Variables

Variables that are kept constant or monitored to minimize/not affect their influence on the outcome.

Example:
Controlling for socioeconomic status in education studies.

- ##### Confounding Variable

A variable that influences both the independent and dependent variables, causing a false association.

Example:
A third variable like IQ affecting both the choice of school and student performance.

- ##### Exogenous Variable

Originates outside the model and is not affected by the other variables in the system. It is always an independent variable. An IV is not always an Exogenous variable.

- ##### Endogenous Variable

Influenced within the system by other variables. It is a dependent variable and vice versa.

### Data Types

- ##### Categorical (Qualitative)

Variables that represent categories (gender, profession)

- ##### Discrete (Quantitative)

Variables that are counted and take specific values (number of calls received)

- ##### Continuous (Quantitative)

Variables that can take all possible values in a range (income, age)

### Formulation of Hypothesis

- A hypothesis is an assumption about statistical parameter. (avg mileage of TVS Jupiter is 65, veg = non veg in MH)

- A hypothesis is a specific, testable prediction derived from a theoretical framework or research question. It proposed a relationship between two or more variables.

#### Types of Hypothesis

1. ##### Null Hypothesis $H_0$

No relationship between variables.

2. ##### Alternative Hypothesis $H_1$

A relationship or effect **exists**. (there is a significant difference between X & Y)

3. ##### Directional Hypothesis

Predicts the direction of the relationship. (X increases Y)

4. ##### Non-Directional Hypothesis

Predicts a relationship but not the direction.

#### Steps in formulating a Hypothesis

1. Identify key variables
2. Review related literature.
3. Formulate a testable prediction.
4. Ensure clarity and simplicity.

eg. **Research Question**: Does physical exercise reduce anxiety among college students?
**Hypothesis**: Students who engage in regular physical exercise report significantly lower levels of anxiety than those who do not.

#### Research Question

A Research question is a clearly defined query that the research seeks to andwer. It sets the direction and scope of a study and is derived from reviewing the literature, identifying gaps, or analyzing practical problems.

##### Characteristics of a good research question

- Clear and Focused
- Feasible to answer
- Empirically testable
- Relevant and significant to the field

##### Types of Research Questions

- Descriptive: What is happening?
- Comparative: What is the difference between A and B?
- Casual: Does X cause Y?

##### Relationship between Research Questions and Hypothesis

- Research Question provides a broad inquiry.
- Hypothesis narrows it down into testable statement.
- A good research design aligns both logically and methodologically.
