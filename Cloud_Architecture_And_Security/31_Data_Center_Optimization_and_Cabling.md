#### Cabling

##### Fibre Optics

- Higher Bandwidth
- Longer distances
- Immunity to electromagnetic interference.

##### Copper Cabling

- Lower Cost
- Easier installation
- Shorter Distances

##### Best Practices

- Labeling
- proper routing
- avoid bending
- use cable trays
- use appropriate cable ties
- avoid over tightening
- regular inspections & maintenance

##### TIA/EIA-942

TIA/EIA-942 is a standard for data center cabling infrastructure.

- Benefits: Scalability, Flexibility, Reliability, Consistency
- It defines cable types, distances, pathways and physical security

###### Site Location & Architecture

- provides guidelines for selecting an appropriate site for a data center.
- design recommendations: Layout, Space allocation, Security zones

###### Electrical Systems

- Defines power distribution requirements.
- covers UPS systems, backup generators and grounding practices.

###### Mechanical Components

- Includes temperature & humidity ranges, airflow management, and cooling redundancy
- Raised floor, hot/cold aisle containment and fire suppressions systems also addressed.

###### Fire Safety

- Fire detection & suppression systems specified.
- Emergency evacuation plans & fire drills are part of the safety measures.

###### Telecommunication Infrastructure

- Covers structured cabling systems, fiber optic and copper cabling, and pathways for data and voice communications.

###### Security and Monitoring

- Physical security: Access control, surveillance, intrusion detections
- Monitoring: Environmental conditions, power usage and equipment status.

###### System Redundancy and Availability

- Promotes redundancy: dual power feeds, diverse network paths, redundant hardware
- Availability Tiers (1 to 4) classify data centers based on uptime and reliability.
